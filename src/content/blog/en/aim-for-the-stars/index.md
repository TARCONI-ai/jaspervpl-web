The first time I heard about this book was one morning at university, in a psychology class. The lecturer, whose name my memory has decided to erase, recommended it and promised it would change our lives.

*Apunta a las estrellas y llegarás a la luna* (Aim for the stars and you will reach the moon, only published in Spanish) is the autobiography of Leopoldo Fernández Pujals. Many of you will not know who he is, but Leopoldo is one of the most successful entrepreneurs in the world: he founded Telepizza and rescued Jazztel from bankruptcy. He is the only Spanish businessman who has taken two companies onto the Ibex 35.

The book opens with a prologue by his wife, Marilina, in which she tells how they met. I found it very interesting because it is unexpected and a lovely love story.

Leopoldo wrote the book to document what he achieved so that his children could follow in his footsteps. He starts by telling how he left Cuba as a child after Fidel Castro came to power, and how his family had to start from scratch in the United States.

The first part of the book is a critique of the impoverishment of his home island and of the oppression the Castro brothers imposed on its people.

Throughout the book, the author stresses the good habits his parents taught him. Good habits are the secret of success in life and in business. Leopoldo also praises the way his parents raised him: they encouraged him to read, which broadened his knowledge and helped his career.

Most of the book tells the story of his professional life, from his start as a salesman at Procter & Gamble. Several chapters cover what he achieved at Johnson & Johnson, which together read like a complete manual on running a sales department.

His successes come from good habits, firm determination and working harder than anyone else, which made him stand out from his colleagues.

From a young age, Leopoldo dreamed of starting a business. That is how he founded Telepizza. It is fascinating to read how Telepizza grew from its first days to its expansion. All of this comes with a business drama: his brother conspired to push him out of the company, which turned out to be a motivation rather than a setback. After taking the company onto the Ibex 35, he sold his shares in Telepizza at the end of 1999.

**If you surround yourself with stars, you will shine; if you surround yourself with mud, you will get muddy.**

I found this book very interesting. I knew nothing about the history of Telepizza, and it is the first book I have read about entrepreneurship and personal development. It is an easy read, and if you are interested in business or simply curious, you may enjoy it. I have left out some very curious chapters on purpose, because I hope you discover the book and read it yourself. I am sure it will motivate you.
