Eduard Punset interviewed Dan Ariely in 2008, after the publication of his book *Predictably Irrational* (in Spanish, "Las trampas del deseo"). The programme looks at how irrationally people behave, as opposed to the classic view of us as rational beings.

The interview starts from how hard it is for consumers to choose any product in the supermarket. We all have a reservation price, and a price we are not willing to pay. Take a restaurant menu: if we see a very expensive burger and another one that is cheaper but still feels affordable, we end up choosing the second one.

This is what we call the reservation price: we know what we are going to pay, and if someone asks for a bit more, we will not pay it. That is why it is so important to study consumer behaviour, a field I am personally very curious about.

As consumers, we believe we buy rationally, but that is not true. In fact, we are quite irrational, and that is where the traps of the market come in. One example of irrational buying is going to the supermarket hungry: need gives way to craving, and we end up buying too much (I include myself).

**Reality is merely an illusion, albeit a very persistent one.**

To make a solid case for irrational buying, the interview shows a street experiment with a chocolate stand. The stand offered two kinds of chocolate: a high-quality one in nice wrapping that cost one cent, and a cheaper-looking one that was free.

The results were more than surprising: 70% of people chose the free product, even though it was worse chocolate, and the remaining 30% paid one cent for the premium one.

And you, what does "free" do to you?
