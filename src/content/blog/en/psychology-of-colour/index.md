The human brain unconsciously links each colour to a range of feelings and mental associations. The study of these mechanisms, which may be innate or shaped in some way by the societies we live in, is called colour psychology.

That is why the choice of a colour can be decisive in a company's non-verbal communication. Think of a logo, the distinctive symbol that goes with every product and advert of a brand. Strong shades such as red or yellow usually stand for liveliness, energy and initiative. Softer ones, such as blue or green, stand for security and trust.

Colour is an incredibly powerful tool for marketers. It can convey emotions and trigger responses in consumers. The colours used in marketing campaigns have a big influence on how people respond to marketing material, so it is important for marketers to know how colours affect consumer behaviour.

It has been shown that the use of colour in advertising has affected consumer behaviour for as long as advertising has existed. A fun fact: Coca-Cola was one of the first brands to bet on colour. From the late 19th century it used red in its advertising, when it was still sold as a medicinal tonic.

Because colour perception is so fundamental, a brand needs to know exactly who its target audience is and how it wants that audience to perceive it. Colours and colour combinations are very important for the success or failure of a brand.
